She Never Knew

There was nothing remarkable about the day I met her.

No dramatic music. No lightning strike. No moment that made the world stop spinning.

She simply smiled at me while asking a question I don't even remember.

But somehow, I remembered the smile.

Days became weeks. Weeks became months.

We talked about ordinary things—movies, bad weather, favorite songs, dreams that felt too distant to say out loud. I learned the way she laughed when she was genuinely happy and how she tucked a loose strand of hair behind her ear when she was nervous.

And somewhere between those small moments, I fell in love.

Not all at once.

Slowly.

Quietly.

The kind of love that grows in silence.

"Some feelings become so comfortable in hiding that they never learn how to be spoken."

I told myself there would be a perfect time to say it. A perfect day. A perfect moment when the words would finally leave my chest and find their way to her.

But life kept moving.

There was always another conversation. Another tomorrow.

Then one afternoon, she showed me a photo.

A smile brighter than I'd ever seen.

A ring on her finger.

"I said yes," she whispered.

I smiled too.

I congratulated her.

And somehow, that hurt more than any rejection ever could.

That night, I lay awake replaying every memory, every almost-confession, every moment I could have spoken.

The words were still there.

"I love you."

Untouched.

Unheard.

Years have passed since then.

Sometimes I wonder what would have happened if I had been brave enough.

Maybe she would have said yes.

Maybe she would have said no.

But the truth is, I'll never know.

Because some stories don't end with heartbreak.

They end with silence.

And she never knew that for the longest time, she was the first thought in my morning and the last one before I slept.

She never knew.

The words lived only in my chest.
