The Last Message

The message sat unfinished on my screen while rain traced slow paths down the window.

"Hey, I still think about you."

Eight words. One year, three months, and eighteen days of silence.

My thumb hovered over the send button. The cursor blinked beneath the text, steady and impatient, like a heartbeat asking for a chance.

We hadn't spoken in 473 days.

The last conversation ended with a simple "seen" — a tiny word that somehow felt heavier than goodbye. Since then, I'd replayed old messages like scenes from a film, searching for clues in places where none existed.

I rewrote the message again.

"How have you been?"

Delete.

"I miss talking to you."

Delete.

"Did you ever think about me too?"

Delete.

Every version felt too much. Or not enough.

"Sometimes the most honest words are the ones we never send."

Outside, the rain grew louder. Inside, the silence felt familiar.

Then a strange thought crossed my mind: maybe I wasn't waiting for a reply. Maybe I was waiting for permission to move on.

I stared at the screen one last time.

"Hey, I still think about you."

For a moment, I imagined the message delivered. The notification. The typing bubble. The answer I'd been secretly hoping for.

Then I imagined nothing.

And somehow, that felt more honest.

I selected the words, pressed delete, and watched them disappear.

The screen went blank.

The rain kept falling.

For the first time in 473 days, I locked my phone without looking back.

Sometimes closure isn't the message they receive.

It's the moment you stop waiting for the reply that was never coming.

And sometimes, the most important goodbye is the one that remains unsent.
