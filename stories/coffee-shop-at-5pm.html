Coffee Shop at 5pm

The coffee shop was exactly as I remembered.

The same wooden tables. The same soft music drifting through the speakers. The same scent of roasted coffee hanging in the air.

Only we were different.

I arrived five minutes early.

You arrived right on time.

Three years had passed since we'd last seen each other.

Three years of missed birthdays, unsent messages, and wondering how the other person was doing.

You smiled when you saw me.

I smiled back.

Then we sat down across from each other.

And suddenly, neither of us knew where to begin.

The waiter took our order.

Two coffees.

Just like before.

The silence settled between us, not awkward, not comfortable—just heavy.

Heavy with everything we never said.

I wanted to ask if you were happy.

I wanted to tell you about all the nights I almost called.

I wanted to apologize for the way things ended.

But every sentence felt years too late.

"Some people leave your life, but their absence keeps finding ways to stay."

You stirred your coffee slowly.

I watched the spoon circle the cup.

Round and round.

Like we were both searching for words at the bottom.

Outside, people hurried past the windows.

Inside, time seemed unwilling to move.

For a brief moment, our eyes met.

And there it was.

Everything.

The laughter.

The memories.

The mistakes.

The goodbye neither of us had wanted.

Yet neither of us spoke.

Because some conversations happen long before words arrive.

And some endings don't need explaining.

The coffees grew cold.

The evening light faded.

Five o'clock became six.

You glanced at the time and stood up.

I stood too.

We exchanged one final smile.

No promises.

No questions.

No "see you again."

Just a quiet understanding.

You walked toward the door.

I watched you leave.

The bell above the entrance rang once.

Then the coffee shop returned to its ordinary noise.

We sat across from each other for an hour and never said a word.

Yet somehow, it felt like we'd said goodbye all over again.
