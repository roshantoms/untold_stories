The Train Ride Home

The station looked smaller than I remembered.

Maybe that happens when years pass.

Places stay the same size, but memories keep growing.

I stood on Platform 3 with a ticket in my pocket and nowhere in particular on my mind.

The evening train was running late.

It always was.

That's where I first met you.

Not in some grand, unforgettable moment.

Just two strangers waiting for the same delayed train.

You were reading a book.

I pretended to read mine.

After twenty minutes of silence, you looked up and asked if the train was always this late.

It was the simplest question anyone had ever asked me.

And somehow, it changed everything.

One conversation became another.

One train ride became dozens.

Soon, Platform 3 felt less like a station and more like a meeting place designed just for us.

We learned each other's coffee orders.

We shared headphones.

We talked about impossible dreams and futures that felt certain.

And somewhere between departures and arrivals, I fell in love.

"The strange thing about first love is that you don't realize it's a memory until it's already over."

The years that followed felt endless.

At least, they did while we were living them.

Then came the day you told me you were leaving.

A new city.

A new opportunity.

A new life.

I smiled and said I was happy for you.

You smiled and said we'd stay in touch.

Both of us knew neither sentence was completely true.

The final train arrived just before sunset.

We stood together on the platform we'd shared for years.

The announcement echoed overhead.

The doors opened.

And suddenly there was nothing left to say.

You hugged me.

I held on a second longer than I should have.

Then you stepped onto the train.

The doors closed.

The train pulled away.

And I watched until the last carriage disappeared around the bend.

That was years ago.

Since then, I've taken countless trains.

I've traveled to different cities, met different people, and built a life I once couldn't imagine.

But every now and then, I find myself back at Platform 3.

Not physically.

Just in memory.

Because some part of me never boarded the next train.

Some part of me is still standing there, watching you leave.

Waiting in the golden light of a station that no longer exists.

People say first love teaches you how to fall.

I think it teaches you how to remember.

And after all these years, I finally understand something.

You left that station long ago.

I did too.

But the version of us that laughed on Platform 3, sharing dreams between delayed trains, never went anywhere.

It stayed behind.

First love and the station we never left.
